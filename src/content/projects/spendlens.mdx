## The problem

Every existing personal finance tool for Indian credit cards wants your
bank statements uploaded to their servers. That's a non-starter for
anything touching real financial data — the convenience isn't worth the
exposure. On top of that, no platform gives a holistic view across cards:
each bank only analyzes its own statements, and even CRED — which comes
closest — still divides everything by card, with too much to sift through
to see one clear picture of total spend.

## The approach

Built a fully local-first analyzer: drop in your bank statement PDFs or
CSVs from any card, get one unified local dashboard. Parsing, storage, and
analysis all run client-side.

## Decisions

- Chose local-only architecture over a hosted SaaS model, trading
  convenience (no access-anywhere) for zero data exposure — the right
  tradeoff when the data is financial
- Built one unified cross-card view instead of a per-card breakdown, since
  every existing option (bank apps, CRED) silos spending by card and never
  shows total spend across all of them together
- Indian bank statement formats aren't standardized across issuers, so
  parsing had to handle inconsistent layouts rather than a single fixed
  template

## Outcome

![SpendLens category breakdown](/images/projects/spendlens/categories.png)

A working dashboard tracking 26 months and 8 cards, with category-level
insights (e.g. surfacing a 258% month-over-month rise in Shopping spend)
that would take manual spreadsheet work to spot otherwise.
